Goal:

Implement a bounded hazard-model confidence and source-provenance layer so operational claims about a hazard, weakness, trigger, origin, source family, or countermeasure carry inspectable confidence and provenance instead of becoming truth by default.

Scope:

- support hazard-model states such as confirmed, suspected, inferred, contested, internally fabricated, externally planted, disinformation, partial-access, source-blocked, source-unverified, source-verified, and superseded
- support source provenance fields such as source type, access level, body access, page/citation confidence, extraction method, source tradition, authoring context, source age, translation status, chain of custody, and corroboration count
- support graded confidence for witness memory, staged media, promotional media, reenactment, folklore, dream testimony, occult tradition, technical report, official archive, field observation, and mixed-source bundles
- support confidence debt where a model is usable for planning but remains risky because source access is blocked, partial, outdated, contradictory, translated, or page-unverified
- support provenance before applying weakness reads, hazard predictions, route assumptions, protective-object claims, or source-derived countermeasures
- support visible and invisible rule correspondence so warnings, meters, statuses, and map marks preserve what the visible signal corresponds to, such as observed fact, instrument reading, staff inference, agency doctrine, stale record, rumor, or deliberately uncertain projection
- support confidence-coded correspondence on visible markers so operational markers remain useful while carrying confidence debt
- support reliable, contextual, or hybrid downstream-use tagging for evidence and clue bundles where one clue may anchor one question while remaining contested for another
- distinguish hazard-model confidence from anomaly taxonomy, document authenticity, evidence custody, record-integrity attacks, and knowledge-state freshness
- expose warnings when a hazard model relies on title-only metadata, blocked-source summaries, unverified scans, page-uncertified claims, or deliberately maintained disinformation

Constraints:

- deterministic only
- no assumption that a cited or official source is true
- no assumption that source confidence equals document authenticity or physical custody quality
- do not duplicate anomaly compendium taxonomy; this issue tracks confidence/provenance of the model, not canonical anomaly class
- do not duplicate document-authenticity analysis owned by JamesJedi420/containment-protocol#1210
- do not duplicate evidence custody and retention owned by JamesJedi420/containment-protocol#874
- do not duplicate record falsification / attack modeling owned by JamesJedi420/containment-protocol#1967
- caseboard organization remains with JamesJedi420/containment-protocol#906; action unlocks remain with JamesJedi420/containment-protocol#1702; UI transformation remains with JamesJedi420/containment-protocol#588; map artifact presentation remains with JamesJedi420/containment-protocol#1253
- keep confidence state, provenance, warning reason, and downstream-use risk legible for audit, authoring review, and player-facing explanation where appropriate

Acceptance criteria:

- at least one hazard model is marked confirmed, one suspected/inferred, and one contested or disinformation-bearing
- at least one source record carries blocked or partial access and therefore cannot be promoted to confirmed runtime fact without corroboration
- at least one weakness or countermeasure claim can be used with confidence debt rather than treated as proven
- at least one mixed-source bundle preserves conflicting provenance and graded confidence instead of collapsing into one verdict
- at least one visible marker preserves correspondence type and confidence debt rather than presenting as observed fact by default
- at least one clue bundle preserves reliable versus contextual downstream-use tagging
- validation or examples distinguish source provenance from document authenticity, evidence custody, and anomaly taxonomy
- targeted tests or validation examples cover deterministic confidence classification, source-access warnings, provenance projection, and downstream-use risk reporting

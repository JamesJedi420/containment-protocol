# Anatomy-Aware Crit and Fumble Resolution (SPE-165)

## Purpose

Severe outcomes (crits, fumbles) are **anatomy-aware**: **nonhumanoid, large, or unusual bodies** use reusable injury and fumble families filtered through **plausibility**, not a single humanoid crit table.

## Body-plan filters

Body plans define:

- locomotion (biped, quadruped, slither, hover),
- limb count and arrangement,
- vital cluster locations,
- size and mass bands.

These filter which crit and fumble outcomes are legal.

## Injury families

Reusable injury families include:

- **persistent functional injury** — vision loss, limb compromise, breath control issues,
- **movement failure** — falls, pinning, stagger, vehicle crash,
- **self-harm / ally harm** — ricochets, friendly fire, tool blowback.

Families map differently for different plans (e.g., tail vs arm vs wing).

## Fumble families

Fumbles may produce:

- dropped or jammed tools,
- overextension or lock-up,
- collateral damage to environment,
- posture compromise that interacts with abilities (SPE-164).

Outcomes that do not fit the body plan must be **remapped or vetoed** according to rules, not ignored ad hoc.

## Integration

- **Aggregate battle & local confrontation** — reference these families for severe outcomes.
- **Staged ability resolution (SPE-164)** — misfire routing may reuse injury/fumble families.\n

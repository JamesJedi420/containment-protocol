# Appraisal-Driven Emotion and Goal Priority Model (SPE-161)

## Purpose

Actors carry **active and protective goals** and derive **emotion states** from **appraisal** of events and state — not fixed triggers or a one-dimensional alignment. Emotions alter **priorities, staffing fitness, and long-horizon motivation**.

## Active vs protective goals

- **Active goals** — what the actor tries to achieve (mission, promotion, revenge, research).
- **Protective goals** — what they try to avoid or preserve (family safety, reputation, doctrine compliance).

Goal sets may conflict; priority resolution is influenced by emotional appraisal.

## Appraisal-based emotion

Emotions are functions of:

- perceived threat vs resource,
- perceived injustice, betrayal, or gratitude,
- belonging and exclusion signals,
- success or failure against expectations.

Intensity bands (e.g., mild, moderate, severe) affect how strongly emotions reshape priorities.

## Belonging and symbolic pressure

Actors experience pressure from:

- affiliation (faction, house, unit),
- role expectations,
- oaths and vows (SPE-146),
- unseen patrons or apex authorities (SPE-81).

Belonging pressure pushes protective goals into focus or suppresses disloyal actions.

## Artificial / anomalous agents

Non-human or constructed actors can still implement **functional emotion states** — bounded variables that:

- re-weight goals,
- adjust risk tolerance,
- or gate protocol choices,

without necessarily matching human phenomenology.

## Staffing and long-horizon evolution

Emotion and goals affect:

- staffing fit (who thrives or burns out),
- assignment quality,
- susceptibility to corruption or withdrawal (SPE-79),
- career arcs and veteran drift (SPE-60).

Long-horizon evolution should be deterministic from key events and appraisals.

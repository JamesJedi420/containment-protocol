# Civic Jurisdiction, Detention, and Unrest Pressure (SPE-87)

## Purpose

**Civic order** is **layered**: multiple **enforcement bodies**, **jurisdictions**, and **response patterns** interact — not a single “police pressure” scalar.

## Enforcement layering

- **Body differentiation** — municipal police, port authority, federal investigators, military MPs, private security, faction wardens — each with scope, ROE, and corruption or reliability tags.
- **Jurisdiction** — overlapping maps determine who may act where; conflicts produce delays, turf fights, or selective enforcement.
- **Response pattern** — public-order vs criminal investigation vs counterterror vs anomaly containment overlays.

## Detention sorting

Detention is **sorted by status and institution**:

- holding vs charged vs administrative,
- civilian vs contractor vs operative,
- medical vs disciplinary vs classified.

Sorting changes clocks, access to counsel, and leak risk.

## Administrative sites as mechanical nodes

Courthouses, permit offices, and detention facilities are **nodes** with queues, bribery surfaces, and document throughput — leverage points for missions and pressure maps.

## Unrest overlays

Unrest modifies **route/access pressure**: curfews, street closures, permit suspensions, checkpoint density. Unrest stacks with **crisis overlays** (riot, strike, blackout) that change which enforcement body is active.

## Coercive process ladders

Model **stop → search → detain → charge** (or local equivalents) as **explicit ladders** with branch conditions, witness risk, and legitimacy deltas.

## Asymmetric treatment

- **Outsider-hostility** — non-locals face higher search rates or worse detention conditions.
- **Crowd dynamics** — density changes escalation thresholds.
- **Protected classes** — diplomatic, medical, or press credentials alter ladder legality.

## Integration

- **Urban service nodes (SPE-86)** — venue rules intersect with enforcement sweeps.
- **Legitimacy** — visible brutality or failed due process shifts standing.

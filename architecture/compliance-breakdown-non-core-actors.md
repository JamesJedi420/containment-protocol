# Compliance, Breakdown, and Non-Core Actor Behavior (SPE-56)

## Purpose

This document defines how **contractors, auxiliaries, civilians, attached specialists, and stressed core teams** behave under institutional pressure. Containment Protocol assumes **bounded obedience**, not automatic compliance outside the narrow core operator path.

## Design rule

If a doc or system still treats non-core participants as **reliably compliant** “extra hit points,” it misstates operational behavior. Compliance is **state-conditioned** and **deterministic from explicit inputs** (pressure, leadership, trust, incentives, doctrine fit, treatment history, reporting fear).

## Non-core and stressed states

Under authored thresholds, actors may enter (non-exhaustive):

- **Hesitation** — delayed execution, reduced initiative, requests for clarification or legal review.
- **Refusal** — lawful or unlawful stand-down; may block procedures or equipment use.
- **Rout** — loss of cohesion; withdrawal without orders; abandonment of posts.
- **Panic** — acute loss of control; erratic actions that spike exposure or collateral.
- **Concealment** — hiding injury, stress, substance use, or policy violations from reporting chains.
- **Acute breakdown** — medical, psychological, or mutiny-equivalent collapse that propagates.

States should be **mutually exclusive per channel where needed** but can **stack** as separate tags (e.g., hesitation + concealment).

## Inputs that shape obedience

Model these as first-class modifiers, not flavor:

- **Leadership shock** — chain-of-command disruption, loss of key NCO or liaison, or contradictory orders.
- **Trust** — toward agency, toward team lead, toward local population or contractors.
- **Incentives** — pay, hazard bonus, immunity, career protection, blackmail leverage.
- **Doctrine fit** — whether current orders match training, ROE, or sponsor policy.
- **Treatment history** — prior abandonment, cover-ups, or sacrifice of auxiliaries changes future compliance.
- **Reporting fear** — whistleblower risk, reprisal, or regulatory exposure suppresses honest signals.

## Institutional side effects

Include in the compliance model (not optional narrative):

- **Collapse propagation** — one rout or refusal mechanically stresses adjacent units (coverage holes, clock pressure).
- **Caregiver / compassion breach risk** — medical or humanitarian actors forced into containment violence; long-tail legitimacy and trauma costs.
- **Regulatory-burden backfire** — excessive paperwork, oversight, or disclosure rules that slow truth ascent and increase concealment.

## Integration

- **Mission resolution** consumes compliance posture as readiness, weakest-link, or fallout inputs.
- **Legitimacy / factions** may shift when auxiliaries are mishandled or scapegoated.
- **Reports** should surface deterministic compliance causes, not ad hoc blame text.

# Emergency Governance and Crackdown States (SPE-147)

## Purpose

Cities can enter **temporary emergency rule** under explicit deterministic thresholds. Emergency governance changes access, detention scope, and patrol posture **without** implying a permanent police-state by default.

## Activation

Emergency states activate when threat / unrest / infiltration clocks cross declared thresholds:

- mass casualty events,
- repeated sabotage,
- evidence of hidden civic understructure (SPE-154),
- sponsor pressure or legitimacy collapse.

Activation must record:

- triggering clocks,
- issuing authority,
- declared duration or review cadence.

## Active-state behavior (examples)

Emergency state may alter:

- **Curfew and gates**: closed districts, checkpoints, permit requirements.
- **Taxes and extraction**: emergency levies, requisitions, seizure rules.
- **Patrol powers**: stop/search ladders widen; surveillance increases.
- **Detention scope**: sorting expands; administrative holds lengthen.
- **Civil-rights handling**: press access, assembly restrictions, credential privileges.

Treat these as **policy overlays** that feed routing, encounter generation, and service-node rule enforcement.

## Restoration to normal

Emergency governance ends through:

- explicit countdown expiry,
- successful stabilization milestones,
- political reversal,
- or catastrophic lock-in (rare, authored).

Restoration must be modeled as a transition with aftereffects (trust loss, paperwork backlog), not a silent flag flip.

## Integration

- **SPE-87 civic governance**: crackdown is an overlay on jurisdiction ladders, not a parallel law system.
- **SPE-109 / SPE-139**: district cadence and urban encounters must consume crackdown overlays.

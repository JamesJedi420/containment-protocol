# Stage-conditioned site generation

Synced from Linear Containment Protocol project resource (2026-05-12). Git is canonical for ongoing edits. Original: [Linear doc](https://linear.app/spectranoir/document/stage-conditioned-site-generation-f869de198197).

## Scope

Covers downstream stage-conditioned site generation after a purpose packet has been chosen, including ingress semantics, entrance placement, nonlethal progression gating, long-range forced movement, and layered stage handoff behavior.

## Core rules

- Site generation proceeds through ordered stages where earlier structural choices condition later outputs.
- Purpose, builder, location, ingress, topology, hazards, treasure, and inhabitants should not behave like disconnected random rolls.
- Outputs should remain explainable enough that an author can tell why a room, route, trap, or inhabitant exists.

## Ingress semantics

- Ingress type is a first-class authored surface, not just a coordinate.
- Doors, shafts, passages, magical entrances, deceptive weather crossings, drifting trails, or apparent shelter routes can all imply different interior expectations and security logic.
- Some ingress types may be passive or deceptive transfer channels where the threshold crossing is not perceived as a declared portal event.
- Exit suppression can use repeated route confusion or environmental interference so investigators may lose ordinary geography without understanding when the transition happened.

## Entrance placement by navigational style

- Entrance placement may depend on navigational complexity.
- Edge ingress supports directional penetration and linear progress.
- Central ingress supports maze-like or radial exploration.
- Later added entrances should not invalidate the original navigational logic.

## Nonlethal progression gates

- Hazards do not need to deal direct damage to matter.
- Nonlethal trap families may immobilize, mislead, redirect, confuse, isolate, or temporarily seal rooms against sound and egress.
- Some rooms may convert into kill sites through poison web, paralysis air, burrowers, dissolving insects, or comparable saturation hazards without relying on a direct monster body.
- Recovery spaces may invert into dependence or entrapment over repeated use rather than staying monotonically beneficial.

## Long-range and vertical forced movement

- Forced movement may cross levels, trigger one-way ejection states, or reposition targets through large vertical or horizontal displacement.
- Terrain, stairs, ledges, pulleys, moats, bridges, wells, swamps, and living entanglement beds remain part of this movement logic.
- Vertical hazards can change who or what can enter, retreat, mount, carry, or survive route transitions.

## Distinctions to preserve

- Stage-conditioned generation is not a full architect simulator.
- Ingress semantics are not just flavor text over a fixed map.
- Nonlethal gates are not weaker damage traps; they change route state, access state, or timing.
- Forced movement is not limited to short same-level knockback.

## Non-goals

- No full maze simulator.
- No sprawling graph-theory or architecture simulator.
- No full physics simulator.
- No assumption that site-generation tables are independent.

## Expected use

Use this doc when defining how a site’s chosen purpose flows forward into ingress type, route grammar, trap style, placement logic, and movement consequences before full encounter detail is layered on top.

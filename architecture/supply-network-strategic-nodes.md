# Supply Network, Transport, and Strategic Nodes (SPE-72)

## Purpose

**Supply** is traced through **nodes, links, transport assets, and block reasons** — not ambient “support pressure.” Unsupported agencies suffer **multiple downstream behavior changes** (readiness, procurement, mission options, morale).

## Network model

- **Sources** — depots, sponsors, black-market brokers, captured sites, or foreign ports.
- **Nodes** — ports, railheads, customs yards, ferry terminals, airfields, ritual transfer points.
- **Links** — edges with capacity, latency, legality flags, and inspection risk.
- **Transport assets** — trucks, barges, aircraft, couriers; each has **vulnerability class** (interdictable, stealth-capable, token-gated).

## Route and modality preferences

- **Sea / river preference** — where campaign geography warrants, prefer water-heavy routing hierarchies with explicit chokepoints (straits, locks, ice).
- **Staged customs intake** — token-validated transit, inspections that delay or corrupt loads, diversion to secondary inspection.
- **Hidden resupply lines** — clandestine paths with exposure if discovered.

## Unsupported state

When supply drops below thresholds or a link severs, apply **unsupported penalties** across systems: reduced kit replenishment, slower recovery, higher procurement friction, or forced mission cancellations — all **source-attributed** in reports.

## Non-cash throughput

Model **non-cash-heavy** flows: barter, salvage rights, favor chits, or sponsor materiel that bypasses raw funding but still consumes **capacity** and **legitimacy**.

## Integration

- **SPE-66 reserves** — reinforcement packages often ride the same network.
- **SPE-65 large assets** — carriers and platforms are both transport and strategic nodes.

## Scope

Covers transformation families, matter-conversion rules, spell modification families, shadow-realness states, and identity-discontinuity survival where body, substrate, illusion, summoning, and continuity of self shift under bounded authored rules.

## Transformation families

- Self-only form shifts, target transformations, scale changes, petrification, chassis replacement, lineage conversion, body-modification horror, and environmental matter conversion may all belong to one broader transformation framework while still using distinct operational rules.
- Transformation must remain mechanical rather than cosmetic, affecting silhouette, locomotion, armor, natural weapons, camouflage, environmental fit, size, vulnerabilities, or upkeep burden.
- Ambient transformation drift and targeted punishment transformations are distinct routes into the same broader family.
- Transformations may be timed, action-locked, and classed by origin such as inherited, transmitted, pathological, curse-derived, or engineered.

## Identity-discontinuity survival

- Some NPCs may survive catastrophe through preserved cognitive substrate, transfer, splitting, replacement chassis, altered embodiment, or other discontinuous continuity states.
- Visible body and continuity of self need not match ordinary assumptions.
- Trust, panic, control, and encounter reading may all change because continuity survives while ordinary embodiment does not.
- Identity-discontinuity is distinct from simple disguise, undeath, or injury alone.

## Matter conversion

- Environmental and object-state transformations should stay distinct from body-only transformations.
- Some systems may normalize arbitrary inputs into one internal payload format before execution, with output magnitude tied to input mass or source state.
- Converted matter can preserve intermediate forms, altered fragility, or delayed-release behavior rather than acting as a one-step swap.

## Spell modification

- Existing spells need not remain immutable once authored.
- Metamagic-like families may modify range, duration, magnitude, target shape, targeting bands, and randomization behavior through bounded orthogonal parameters.
- Local world-state overrides may redirect, distort, or remap otherwise normal spell behavior.
- Different power families may scale differently, with some using progressive intensity and others binary core effects plus modifiers.

## Shadow realness continuum

- Illusion, summoning, transport, and semi-real constructs can coexist on a continuum of partial materialization rather than binary fake-or-real categories.
- Illusions should first test plausibility in context before disbelief or collapse.
- Additive false surfaces are distinct from true removal or true concealment.
- False constructs may escalate from ordinary illusion into persistent semi-real obstacles and then into uncontrolled materially real entities or hazards.
- Multisensory bounded illusions should support sight, sound, smell, touch, or taste under explicit volume and fidelity limits.
- Shadow-derived transport or summoning may change behavior depending on realness band rather than sharing ordinary portal or summon assumptions.

## Distinctions to preserve

- Transformation is not just presentation swap.
- Metamagic is not ordinary spell selection.
- Shadow realness is not identical to illusion, and not identical to ordinary summoning.
- Material conversion is not the same as body conversion.
- Identity continuity is not guaranteed by body continuity.

## Non-goals

- No universal polymorph sandbox.
- No infinite spell-customization sandbox.
- No universal shadow sandbox.
- No sprawling exception-tree simulator.

## Expected use

Use this doc when mechanics change what a body, spell, substrate, or illusion really is, how real it is, how continuity persists, or which parameter families remain mutable under authored conditions.

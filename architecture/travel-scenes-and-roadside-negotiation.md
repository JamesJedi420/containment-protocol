# Travel scenes and roadside negotiation

## Source

Synced from Linear Containment Protocol project resource (2026-05-12). Git is canonical for ongoing edits. Original: [Linear doc](https://linear.app/spectranoir/document/travel-scenes-and-roadside-negotiation-3a242f8ea0dc).

## Scope

Covers noncombat travel scenes, roadside encounters, route-pressure interactions, socially mixed approach scenes, and NPC mission carriers whose disclosure, trust thresholds, and support value evolve during contact.

## Logistics and deadline roadside scenes

- Travel between locations may generate scenes whose primary pressure comes from cargo weight, broken transport, underpowered draft teams, route length, weather, escort quality, and deadlines rather than enemies.
- The same route should remain modifiable by terrain, urgency, hazard overlays, and remaining time.
- Travel-linked events may be insertable at several phases of movement, including roadside, convoy, waypoint, delay, or delivery windows.

## Morally mixed roadside negotiation

- An apparent antagonist does not need to be wholly evil or wholly safe.
- Encounters may combine coercive charm, local grievance, internal party pressure, and the possibility of converting confrontation into a local contract or intervention task.
- Low-prestige local objects may work as narrow countermeasures against influence or perception threats.
- Seemingly minor intermediaries may later be recontextualized as dead guides, compromised helpers, or partial truths rather than ordinary roadside locals.

## Tone-sensitive trickster branching

- Trickster or toll-like encounters may branch by player tone rather than only by compliance or combat outcome.
- Courtesy, aggression, mockery, and social salience may each produce distinct later effects.
- Positive contact can unlock hidden enclaves, hospitality, gifts, or support.
- Negative contact can trigger delayed harassment, revised local rules, ambush pressure, or changed route assumptions instead of immediate battle.
- Tricksters may teach by negative example, using the player’s repeated habits or assumptions against them on later passes.

## Conditional-disclosure carriers

- Couriers, pilgrims, chroniclers, brokers, or other mission carriers may reveal only partial intent on first contact.
- Deeper disclosure can require trust, companionship, solitary meeting, truthful presentation, escort, or repeated contact.
- Anti-deception responses may route through false guidance or ejection before open confrontation.
- Soft sources may narrow a search space without giving hard proof or precise coordinates.
- Paid clue brokers may sell deliberately encoded hints as a distinct service class.
- Useful asymmetry should remain valid: an ally can provide enough truth for tactical cooperation while still withholding core identity, capability, or mission state.

## Hidden rescuer sanctuary logic

- A concealed protector tied to a private sanctuary may intervene selectively without becoming a universal rescuer.
- Rescue doctrine can remain constrained by territory, taboo, private rules, or personal aversion.
- Shelter decisions may involve real tactical value from an unverified ally whose motives or affiliations remain only partially disclosed.
- Rescue should not automatically collapse uncertainty just because help arrived.

## Distinctions to preserve

- Travel scenes are not empty padding between destinations.
- Charm pressure is not identical to full social sandbox simulation.
- Trickster encounters are not throwaway comic relief.
- Local countermeasures matter precisely because formal responders may dismiss them.
- Mission carriers are not generic rumor givers.
- Hidden rescuers are not deus-ex-machina patrons.

## Non-goals

- No full caravan simulator.
- No universal social reputation simulator.
- No sprawling dialogue-tree system for every travel encounter.
- No universal patron or rescuer simulation.

## Expected use

Use this doc when a route, roadside stop, soft contact, partial informer, or hidden rescuer should change information state, trust thresholds, local obligations, or future support without defaulting to direct combat.

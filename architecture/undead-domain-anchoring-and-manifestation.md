# Undead domain anchoring and manifestation

## Source

Synced from Linear Containment Protocol project resource (2026-05-12). Git is canonical for ongoing edits. Original: [Linear doc](https://linear.app/spectranoir/document/undead-domain-anchoring-and-manifestation-2ca77a8d4e36).

## Scope

Covers undead or spectral entities whose agency is tied to domains, anchors, remains, rites, and manifestation-state changes rather than simple free-roaming hostile behavior.

## Included issue boundary

- SPE-390

## Core rules

- Some undead rulers or domain-scale dead entities remain anchored to a private domain while intermittently projecting into ordinary space.
- Tactical defeat or banishment does not by itself erase the anchor. The entity may reappear at its death, burial, or domain anchor until body-state, remains handling, or rite conditions are corrected.
- Domain anchoring is distinct from both free-roaming undead behavior and one-scene haunt logic.

## Manifestation states

- Manifestation may shift among corporeal, incorporeal, mutable, or semi-corporeal states under explicit rules.
- Different manifestation states can alter resilience, banishment difficulty, movement, and intrusion response.
- A domain-anchored entity may enforce sovereignty without remaining constantly local in one visible body.

## Anchor and remains logic

- Place attachment, neglected remains, ritual sequence, burial procedure, and corpse care remain part of the neutralization model.
- A domain anchor may persist through temporary banishment until the underlying body-state or rite condition is corrected.
- Resolution should not be combat-centric when anchor handling and remains treatment carry equivalent or greater weight.

## Domain-scale consequences

- Failed transcendence or failed escape rituals may convert a city or region into a stable post-disaster undead domain.
- Such domains can establish undead existence as baseline civic reality rather than one isolated dungeon condition.
- Mass conversion, altered social order, and ongoing territorial enforcement may all read from that transformed default state.

## Tactical and strategic behavior

- Some hostile factions may act under resurrection confidence, accepting tactical self-death, low retreat, or self-termination when later restoration is expected.
- Capture logic may include self-termination when interrogation risk outweighs present survival value.
- Some hostile actors may value intact targets for later harvesting more than corpses, altering target priority and confrontation behavior.

## Distinctions to preserve

- Domain anchoring is not identical to ordinary ghost persistence.
- Banishment is not equivalent to permanent removal.
- Undead handling should not default to direct confrontation when anchor and rite conditions dominate outcome.

## Non-goals

- No universal ghost-sovereign model for every undead.
- No sprawling haunt simulator.
- No assumption that undead are either always local or always free-roaming.

## Expected use

Use this doc when authoring undead rulers, anchor-bound manifestations, post-disaster undead regions, or haunt-like entities whose defeat, return, and territorial behavior depend on domain state, remains handling, and ritual correction.

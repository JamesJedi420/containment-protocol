# First-run week loop (onboarding framing)

## Purpose

This note clarifies how the first playable weekly loop should be framed so onboarding does not imply an omniscient agency manager. It also should not imply that the campaign is **forever confined** to a single local geography: **later offsite operations** (SPE-280) can **inherit the same actors, obligations, and canonical world state** without a tacit reset.

## Compact week loop (managerial cadence)

Linear onboarding copy (mirrored here so git stays canonical):

1. **Review the week** — inspect the event feed, candidate pool, current team or company state, available knowledge, and emerging blockers.
2. **Recruit or defer** — expand capacity now or preserve resources for immediate operations.
3. **Equip and validate** — ensure teams meet loadout, certification, readiness, and access requirements before commitments lock in.
4. **Train, recover, and prepare** — restore operators, rebuild shared support where possible, and use rest or prep actions that may also generate bounded next-cycle leverage.
5. **Form teams** — decide who works together; inspect cohesion, coverage, weakest-link risk, and persistent company-level state affecting travel, recovery, access, or logistics.
6. **Triage missions** — choose which incidents or contracts matter now, which can wait, and which risks are acceptable.
7. **Deploy** — commit teams to the work the agency can support this week.
8. **Resolve outcomes** — absorb injuries, costs, fallout, trust movement, escalation, and follow-up consequences.
9. **Recover and progress** — handle downtime, treatment, support, upgrades, and longer-horizon improvements before the next week begins.

## Campaign objective (canonical player-facing framing)

**Objective:** Run a containment agency through escalating weekly pressure. Success means keeping the agency functional from week to week, building a more capable and resilient organization over time, and absorbing paranormal fallout without losing operational control.

This mirrors the canonical strategic success framing in `planning/milestone-label-policy.md` and is the player-facing statement for the first-run loop. Operational detail stays in the weekly loop and the relevant system docs.

### Success at three horizons

| Horizon                               | Success means                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Player-visible evidence                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **One week — stabilization**          | The agency ends the week able to act again: essential teams remain viable, active pressures are contained or bounded, and recovery, staffing, and support are sufficient for the next week.                      | Week report, unresolved incidents, team readiness, recovery and support state.                                   |
| **Several weeks — agency growth**     | Capacity improves rather than merely surviving: the roster, team cohesion, facilities, equipment, knowledge, and standing compound into more options and better risk management.                                 | Roster and team progression, facility and equipment upgrades, research and archive gains, faction standing.      |
| **Long campaign — strategic success** | The agency sustains operations across changing pressure and scale, absorbs paranormal fallout without losing command continuity, and can widen operations without resetting actors, obligations, or world state. | Campaign history, persistent obligations, offsite or scale transitions, continuity of roster and world pressure. |

Success is cumulative and relative to the agency's situation. A week can be stabilizing even when it contains losses; a campaign can be strategically successful without eliminating every threat.

### Failure pressure

Failure is not a single terminal event. It is the cumulative loss of the agency's ability to keep acting under five pressures: **attrition** (deaths, resignations, injuries, and unavailable operators), **downtime** (recovery, treatment, and administrative load consuming the weeks and capacity needed for the next operation), **escalation** (unresolved or mishandled incidents raising the severity, tempo, or scope of future pressure faster than the agency can answer), **trust and standing loss** (faction, civic, or institutional confidence eroding access, cooperation, funding, or license to operate), and **capacity collapse** (facilities, equipment, support, knowledge, or command continuity no longer sustaining the commitments the agency has taken on). These pressures interact: attrition deepens downtime, downtime delays escalation response, and a failed response costs standing and capacity. The campaign becomes degraded when the agency can no longer absorb the next shock without giving up something that matters.

### Minimum surfaces for this framing

The objective and horizons must remain visible through existing surfaces, without adding a parallel victory system:

- **Onboarding and first-run framing** — state the objective and the one-week success condition before the first commitment.
- **Weekly loop and week report** — show whether the week stabilized or degraded the agency and which pressure moved.
- **Progression surfaces** — show growth in roster, teams, facilities, equipment, knowledge, and standing across several weeks.
- **Campaign continuity surfaces** — keep obligations, roster history, and unresolved world pressure visible across scale changes.
- **Failure and recovery messaging** — name the pressure that is accumulating and the recovery path, rather than presenting a single terminal loss.

### Exclusions

This framing does **not** introduce:

- a new victory condition, score, or terminal campaign state;
- a new defeat screen or forced campaign end;
- new simulation rules, balance changes, persistence fields, or UI components;
- a promise that every campaign can reach every horizon, or that losses can always be reversed;
- a replacement for the weekly loop, existing reports, or system-specific success criteria.

This note does not change, add, or remove any campaign-end, defeat, or retirement behavior.

## Success cues (stabilizing vs degraded)

**A stabilizing week** leaves the agency more ready for the next one: key teams remain viable, major pressures are contained, recovery or progression is still possible, and the agency preserves enough support, knowledge, and operational continuity to keep acting.

**A degraded week** leaves the agency narrower and more brittle: readiness drops, injuries or blockers accumulate, support erodes, pressure escalates faster than capacity, and the next week starts from a weaker position.

## Framing rule (player role)

This is a managerial, deterministic, team-first loop. The player is evaluating capacity, preparing teams, choosing commitments, and absorbing consequences rather than directly piloting tactical encounters.

The campaign can later transition into offsite operations without resetting actor, obligation, or world state; the same continuity rules still matter when operational scale changes.

## Core framing rule — command knowledge is partial (SPE-189)

Even when the player “is the agency,” command knowledge is **partial and constrained**:

- **Clearance ceiling** is not the same as **granted access**.
- Room access, file access, and anomaly record access can diverge across actors and offices.
- Some information is **deliberately absent** (redaction, compartment splits, missing archives).

The first-run flow should surface _why_ an answer is missing (“not cleared,” “not granted,” “sealed,” “no precedent,” “archive cut”) rather than pretending the system is hiding nothing.

**Continuity and scale (SPE-280):** the same honesty about partial command knowledge applies when the campaign **migrates offsite**—obligations, roster continuity, and unresolved world pressure **carry forward** unless a deliberately scoped reset is part of the design.

## Where this shows up in the loop

- **Hub / leads**: rumors and clue packets are confidence-bearing and can be wrong.
- **Briefing**: question budgets constrain what can be asked before deployment.
- **Reports**: explanations are computed from canonical state, not invented after the fact.

## Team formation is not only week-local staffing (SPE-191)

Campaign play can maintain **persistent company / party state** distinct from individual operators:

- membership and composition rules,
- group-level records that affect **travel, recovery, access, and logistics**,
- continuity surfaces that persist across weeks (not just “pick a team for this mission”).

## Recovery can rebuild shared group resources (SPE-192)

Preparation and recovery phases may rebuild or spend **shared team support resources** (bond/cohesion budgets) that are separate from individual health, readiness, or morale.

## Rest can generate bounded next-cycle leverage (SPE-195)

Rest and prep actions can create **temporary foresight / leverage** with explicit expiry and bounded downstream spend (planning-quality improvements, safer commitment windows), not only restore depleted state.

## The first-run loop is not the final scale of the campaign (SPE-280)

Later progression may move operations **offsite** (field offices, annexed sites, extended deployment theaters) **without** resetting **actors**, **obligations**, or **canonical world state**. Onboarding should avoid implying that the initial local weekly cadence is a **closed sandbox** that discards continuity when geography or mission class widens.
